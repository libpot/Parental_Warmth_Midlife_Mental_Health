# Parental_Warmth_Midlife_Mental_Health
early caregiving experiences may shape mental health well beyond adolescence, yet evidence on such long-term associations across populations and their potential psychosocial pathways remains limited. To address these evidence gaps, we applied a causal inference approach to data from population-based cohorts from the United Kingdom and Norway spanning multiple decades (see Figure 1 for an overview of the study design). Our main aim was to examine the relationship between parental warmth assessed in adolescence and mental health across three midlife outcome domains: psychological distress, psychosocial well-being, and substance use, while accounting for adolescent mental health and a broad range of demographic, family socioeconomic, and health-related confounders. Applying a causal mediation approach, we further investigated whether adult social relationships could account for potential protective relationships.
